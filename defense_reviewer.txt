                        1. PROJECT PURPOSE & STATEMENT OF THE PROBLEM
====================================================================================================
* The Core Problem: 
  Junior High School students at SVBSM face modern digital threats (cyberbullying, online harassment)
  and hidden mental distress. The school's traditional approach is reactive and decentralized: it
  relies on manual, paper-based reporting, decentralized report management, and verbal reports.
  This creates massive delays, paper trails, and a failure to intervene during the "Golden Hour"—the
  critical window immediately following a student's expression of distress when support is most
  effective. Parents are often the last to know, and guidance staff are overwhelmed with paperwork.

* StudentShield's Core Purpose:
  To provide SVBSM with a dependable, automated, and centralized platform for real-time risk
  detection and proactive student intervention. By leveraging Natural Language Processing (NLP)
  and automated dual-dispatch alerting (SMS + Email), the system bridges the school-to-home
  communication gap, flags high-stress vulnerabilities instantly, and streamlines counseling workflows.

====================================================================================================
                        2. GENERAL & SPECIFIC OBJECTIVES OF THE STUDY
====================================================================================================
* General Objective:
  To design and develop an NLP-powered monitoring, reporting, and intervention system (StudentShield)
  for SVBSM Junior High School students that automates bullying detection, categorizes emotional
  distress risks, and facilitates proactive, data-driven administrative interventions.

* Specific Objectives:
  1. Secure "Safe Space" Web Portal: Provide Junior High School students with a secure web page to
     conditionally anonymously submit emotional vents and formal bullying incident reports (with options
     to upload photo/video evidence).
  2. Multi-Model AI Risk Classification Engine: Implement mDeBERTa-v3 and DistilRoBERTa to analyze 
     vent texts and classify emotional distress into four tiers: Low, Medium, High, and Critical.
  3. Behavioral Trajectory Tracking: Integrate a vector-based search algorithm (FAISS) to analyze 
     a student's 14-day history and identify worsening trajectories of cumulative distress over time.
  4. Real-Time Alerting System: Build automated parent and counselor notification gateways via SMS 
     (using Semaphore SMS API or Local Android SMS Gateway) and Email (using secure SMTP via Gmail).
  5. Security & Legal Compliance: Protect minor student disclosures using AES-256 data-at-rest 
     encryption, password hashing (bcrypt), and validate system accuracy through pilot testing with the
     SVBSM administration in compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173).

====================================================================================================
                        3. SIGNIFICANCE OF THE STUDY & TARGET BENEFICIARIES
====================================================================================================
* Client School (SVBSM): Replaces vulnerable paper record storage with a secure, centralized database
  compliant with DepEd rules. Streamlines the guidance counselor's daily workload.
* Students: Provides an inviting, non-threatening digital safe space where they can vent or report
  incidents anonymously, removing the fear of peer retaliation or initial judgment.
* Guidance Counselors: Virtual assistant that filters through hundreds of vents, prioritizing
  "High" and "Critical" cases so they can focus clinical support where it is urgently needed.
* Parents/Guardians: Fills the communication gap. Parents are immediately alerted via automated SMS
  and email during life-threatening crises, allowing home intervention to start alongside school protocols.
* The Proponents: Deepened experience in combining PHP/CodeIgniter 4 backend with a Python/FastAPI
  machine learning service, secure API keys, and AES-256 cryptographic protocols.
* Legal Alignment: Directly helps the school comply with Republic Act No. 10627 (Anti-Bullying Act 
  of 2013) and Republic Act No. 11036 (Philippine Mental Health Act).

                        4. THE TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE & DECOUPLED STACK
====================================================================================================
StudentShield uses a decoupled, hybrid architecture to keep the client's operational cost low while
maintaining secure, high-performance processing:

* The Web App Layer (Orchestration & UI):
  - Tech Stack: PHP 8.1+ / CodeIgniter 4.4 framework.
  - Database: MySQL 8.0 (using InnoDB engine).
  - Frontend: Tailwind CSS 3.0 & Vanilla JavaScript.
  - Role: Manages student profiles, user authentication, counselor dashboards, audit logs, and handles
    database storage. Decouples heavy AI computations to keep the student-facing portal lightweight, 
    fast, and accessible even on slow school networks.
  - Host: Portable to free or cheap hosting environments (e.g., InfinityFree during pilot, scalable
    to AWS, HostGator, or DigitalOcean without changing backend code).

* The Neural Hub Layer (ML Microservice):
  - Tech Stack: Python 3.10+ / FastAPI 0.109 framework.
  - Containers: Runs inside a Docker container for ease of environment configuration and stability.
  - Role: Dedicated microservice that receives submitted text, runs parallel transformer pipelines,
    computes distress and emotion matrices, queries historical vectors, and returns structured JSON responses.
  - Host: Hosted on a Microsoft Azure Linux Virtual Machine running on traditional CPUs (low-cost).

* The Security Layer (Strict Cryptography):
  - All sensitive student data (vent texts, bullying incident files) is symmetrically encrypted at
    the application level before database insertion using AES-256-GCM. Raw data only lives in memory
    during an active, authorized counselor's login session. Passwords use bcrypt hashing.

* The Dual-Dispatch Alert Layer:
  - Automated Email: Gmail SMTP protocol using Google App Passwords to push structured HTML alerts.
  - Automated SMS: Integrates both the Semaphore SMS API (cloud) and a Local Android SMS Gateway.
    In the local gateway, the web application pushes HTTP POST requests to an Android phone running an
    SMS gateway app on the school's local network, sending texts to parents without incurring heavy subscription fees.

====================================================================================================
                        5. DEEP DIVE: THE NEURAL MACHINE LEARNING PIPELINE
====================================================================================================
Every time a student submits a vent, the FastAPI service triggers a parallel inference pipeline:

1. Primary Distress Classification (mDeBERTa-v3):
   - Model: Fine-tuned multilingual DeBERTa-v3 (`ashlogg/student-shield-mdeberta`).
   - Why: Multilingual capabilities excel at processing Tagalog-English code-switching ("Taglish"),
     adolescent slang (e.g., "gg," "awit," "slay"), and Gen-Z colloquialisms. Standard monolingual
     models often misclassify these or ignore them as noise.
   - Fail-Safes: If the primary model API fails, the backend immediately routes to fail-safe models
     `SamLowe/roberta-base-go_emotions` and `unitary/toxic-bert`.

2. Granular Emotion Extraction (DistilRoBERTa):
   - Model: `j-hartmann/emotion-english-distilroberta-base`.
   - Why: Extracts probability scores for 7 core emotional states: sadness, fear, anger, disgust,
     joy, surprise, and neutral. This allows the system to differentiate between active anger (raising
     bullying flags) and deep sadness (raising self-harm/distress flags), adjusting weights accordingly.

3. Semantic Crisis Matching (MiniLM):
   - Model: `paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2`.
   - Why: High-dimensional vector generation (384 dimensions) to calculate cosine similarity between
     the student's input and a pre-configured database of clinically significant "Crisis Anchors."
     Crucially, these anchors cover multiple local dialects (English, Tagalog, Ilocano, Bisaya) to
     catch creative suicidal ideation or threat phrases regardless of spelling variations.

4. Contextual Behavioral Tracking (FAISS Vector Memory):
   - Engine: Facebook AI Similarity Search (FAISS).
   - Why: Psychological distress is cumulative; isolated text analysis fails to catch gradual decay.
     FAISS indexes and retrieves the student's historical entries from the last 14 days. By calculating
     the "Trend Velocity" (slope of risk escalation over 14 days), the system dynamically boosts the
     risk score if a student exhibits a steady decline in mental wellness.

5. Risk Classification Tiers:
   - LOW (1% - 30%): Normal emotional release. Stored quietly, no counselor alert.
   - MEDIUM (31% - 60%): Moderate frustration, light verbal insults. Flagged on dashboard.
   - HIGH (61% - 85%): Severe distress or reports of physical fights. Urgent priority dashboard alert.
   - CRITICAL (86% - 100%): Explicit self-harm intent, extreme abuse. Pushes immediate counselor popup,
     sends automated SMTP email, and fires automated SMS to the parents.

                        6. TWO-TIERED SAFETY NET & LOCAL HEURISTIC FALLBACK
====================================================================================================
What if the machine learning service goes down due to internet cuts or server memory limits?
StudentShield utilizes a safety-first "safety net for the safety net":

* Tier 1 (Semantic Analysis): The Python FastAPI microservice runs its deep neural pipeline.
* Tier 2 (Local Heuristic Fallback): Embedded directly in the PHP/CodeIgniter Application Layer.
  - The PHP controller runs a local regex scanner on the raw text *before* encrypting it for database storage.
  - It matches the text against a highly categorized local dictionary of extreme crisis words (e.g.,
    "suicide", "laslas", "bigti", "mamatay", "physical abuse").
* Decision Matrix:
  - If both tiers are operational, the system evaluates the AI-computed risk score against the local
    heuristic risk score and automatically applies the HIGHER of the two.
  - If the Python FastAPI microservice is offline, the PHP layer silently triggers the Heuristic model
    as a complete fallback. A critical entry containing severe self-harm words will STILL fire parent
    SMS alerts and counselor notifications, ensuring zero gaps in crisis response.

                        7. USER JOURNEYS & SYSTEM WORKFLOWS
====================================================================================================
* The Student Journey:
  1. Access: Students log in securely using their School ID and a private passcode.
  2. "Safe Space" Venting: Submit text disclosures in conditional anonymity.
  3. "Silent Panic" Button: Designed for active, immediate danger. A double-tap on a hidden panic UI
     instantly bypasses typing and triggers a Code Red Parent and Counselor SMS alert with their identity.
  4. Post-Vent Grounding UI: Upon submitting highly distressed vents, the interface transitions into
     a beautiful, guided 60-second breathing animation to calm the student during their "Golden Hour."

* The Counselor Journey:
  1. Dashboard Overview: A visual hub displaying school-wide emotional metrics, trends, and hotspots.
  2. AI-Assisted Triage: The counselor reads decrypted vents. To save time during high-volume periods,
     the ML engine outputs automated critical bullet summaries of long vents.
  3. Hotspot Analysis: The NLP engine extracts location names (e.g., "gym", "likod ng building") to
     generate a visual Heatmap of bullying locations, helping the Principal optimize security patrols.
  4. Parent Resolution Loop: 24 hours after a Critical risk alert is fired, the system automatically
     sends a secure verification link to parents ("Has the issue been settled at home?") to collect
     resolutions and close the case log.
  5. Corroboration Logic: All bullying reports are marked as "Pending Review." Counselors must manually
     verify reports before taking action to prevent fake reports or weaponization.

====================================================================================================
                        8. METHODOLOGY, SDLC, SAMPLING, AND STATISTICAL TREATMENT
====================================================================================================
* Methodology: Mixed-methods design evaluating technical performance (accuracy) and user usability.
* Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC): Agile Scrum model.
  - Why Agile: Machine learning systems involve heavy trial-and-error, dataset training adjustments,
    and adaptive logic. Agile's iterative sprints allowed the team to adjust algorithms based on initial
    testing (e.g., correcting sarcasm false positives or local slang weights) before final deployment.
  - Sprints: Sprint 1 (CI4 foundation, UI, encryption); Sprint 2 (FastAPI pipeline, models integration);
    Sprint 3 (FAISS vector and threshold tuning); Sprint 4 (Azure deployment, pilot testing, final checks).
* Sampling Technique:
  - Stratified Random Sampling: Used to select Junior High School student respondents across sections.
    The required sample size is determined using Slovin's Formula to ensure statistical representation.
  - Purposive Sampling: Used to select target guidance counselors and IT administrators based on their
    expert professional roles inside SVBSM.
* Data Gathering Tool: A standardized software evaluation questionnaire adapted from the ISO/IEC 25010
  guidelines (focusing on functional suitability, usability, performance, and security).
* Statistical Treatment:
  - Percentage and Frequency Distribution: Profiles demographics and proportion of user ratings.
  - Weighted Mean: Assesses Likert-scale responses from the ISO/IEC 25010 questionnaire.
  - Confusion Matrix Metrics: Empirically measures the Python ML microservice using Precision, Recall,
    F1-Score, and overall Accuracy against pre-classified synthetic distress test cases.

  - Verify "Reject as Fake" stops parent alerts.

  - Confirm dashboard decrypts text in real-time only inside counselor's active session.

                        10. DREADED PANEL QUESTIONS & ANSWERS (DEFENSE TRAPS)
====================================================================================================
TRAP 1: "Why did you build custom models instead of just calling the OpenAI (ChatGPT) API? It's much simpler."
  * DO NOT say: "Because ChatGPT is hard to code."
  * SAFE ACADEMIC ANSWER: 
    "Using proprietary cloud models like OpenAI presents three critical showstoppers:
     1. Compliance & Security (RA 10173): Sending sensitive emotional disclosures of minor students to
        foreign third-party servers violates the Data Privacy Act of 2012. Our architecture guarantees
        all sensitive data is encrypted locally with AES-256 and processed in our own private VM environment.
     2. Contextual Nuances: Generic cloud models are not fine-tuned for local Taglish code-switching or
        highly localized Gen-Z school slang. Our mDeBERTa model is specifically tuned for this linguistic domain.
     3. Financial Sustainability: A school-deployed platform must run on a sustainable budget. API token costs
        scale rapidly. Our local, CPU-friendly microservice runs on a fixed-cost virtual machine with zero per-token fees."

TRAP 2: "Is your system diagnosing mental illness? What if the AI misclassifies a serious suicidal case?"
  * DO NOT say: "Our AI is 100% accurate and can replace clinical therapists."
  * SAFE ACADEMIC ANSWER: 
    "Absolutely not, Sir/Ma'am. StudentShield is strictly a screening and triaging framework, not a clinical 
    diagnostic tool. The system operates on a 'Human-in-the-Loop' model in strict compliance with UNICEF 2021 
    AI guidelines. The AI acts as a virtual assistant that prioritizes critical cases for human intervention, 
    but all official counselors' decisions and student clinical care are managed manually. To prevent missed cases, 
    we also developed a Local Heuristic Fallback using direct keywords, ensuring that even if the AI misclassifies 
    or goes offline, explicit indicators of self-harm will always be caught."

TRAP 3: "What happens if a student tries to trigger false critical alerts to spam parents or get peers in trouble?"
  * DO NOT say: "We trust our students not to do that."
  * SAFE ACADEMIC ANSWER:
    "We have established robust defense mechanisms against system weaponization:
     1. Bullying reports require Stage 1 manual verification. The counselor must review and verify the report 
        before Stage 2 parent alerts are sent. No automated alerts are sent to the accused student's parents based 
        on raw reports.
     2. Rate limiters restrict submissions (30-sec limit for vents, 120-sec for bullying reports) to block spamming.
     3. The Parent SMS for Critical vents is phrased as a 'high-stress wellness check' rather than an accusation, 
        ensuring safety and prompting standard home check-ins without causing false disciplinary panics."

TRAP 4: "Why did you use mDeBERTa-v3? Why not standard BERT or RoBERTa?"
  * SAFE ACADEMIC ANSWER:
    "DeBERTa-v3 (Decoding-enhanced BERT with Disentangled Attention) introduces ELECTRA-style pre-training, which He et 
    al. (2021) demonstrated beats BERT and RoBERTa on all major classification benchmarks while utilizing far fewer 
    parameters. Multilingual DeBERTa-v3 (mDeBERTa-v3) allows us to run inference with high contextual accuracy using 
    traditional server CPU power. Crucially, its multilingual pre-training is uniquely suited to capture Taglish and 
    Philippine Gen-Z slang, whereas standard BERT models fail on code-switching text."

TRAP 5: "What if a student types sarcasm, e.g., 'KMS (kill myself) because our math quiz is so hard'? Won't this trigger parent panic?"
  * SAFE ACADEMIC ANSWER:
    "This was a key focus during system refinement. The system utilizes multi-dimensional ensemble checks:
     1. DistilRoBERTa extracts emotional dimensions. For casual sarcasm, the fear/sadness vectors remain low 
        while surprise/joy/neutral remain moderate, signaling a hyperbolic tone.
     2. MiniLM vector matching measures the cosine distance against true clinical crisis anchors. Hyperbolic 
        slang expressions do not match the semantic profile of active, planned self-harm.
     3. Sarcasm logic automatically downgrades these to Medium/High (requiring quiet counselor review on the dashboard) 
        rather than triggering a Critical level parent alarm, effectively separating hyperbole from genuine danger."

TRAP 6: "What happens to the data if the school ceases operation or the project terminates? How is data protected?"
  * SAFE ACADEMIC ANSWER:
    "We use industry-standard AES-256 application-level encryption for all sensitive text before saving it to the MySQL 
    database. Password hashes use bcrypt. If a data breach occurs, or if the server hosting environment is compromised, 
    the raw data remains completely unreadable as hexadecimal strings since the decryption keys are stored separately 
    within local environment files. Counselors can also securely soft-delete and purge records, fully aligning with 
    the student's 'Right to be Forgotten' under the Data Privacy Act of 2012."

TRAP 7: "Why did you use Agile methodology for a system with minor modifications?"
  * SAFE ACADEMIC ANSWER:
    "StudentShield is not a simple, static software package. Building a decoupled hybrid system that connects a PHP 
    backend with a Python deep-learning service requires highly interactive iteration. Agile methodology allowed us to 
    develop the core foundation in Sprint 1, integrate complex AI models in Sprint 2, tune the threshold vectors and 
    sarcasm fallbacks in Sprint 3 based on initial evaluation data, and deploy the production-ready build in Sprint 4. 
    This iterative approach prevented architecture failures that standard linear models (like Waterfall) would have missed."

                        11. PRACTICAL ORAL DEFENSE TIPS FOR THE TEAM
====================================================================================================
* Tip 1: Master the "Baton Pass"
  Assign specific expert roles to each student to show unity and avoid speaking over each other:
  - Melgarejo / Ramos (Systems Development & Web Stack): Best suited to answer questions about the PHP 
    CodeIgniter 4 app, database design, local regex fallbacks, SMTP setups, and system deployment.
  - Meneses / Enriquez (AI Integration & Data Science): Handlers of all machine learning questions, model weights, 
    mDeBERTa, DistilRoBERTa, FAISS math vectors, and sarcasm logic.
  - Urayanza (System Usability, Methodology & Compliance): Perfect to present Agile sprints, stratified 
    sampling (Slovin's), ISO/IEC 25010 survey metrics, and RA 10173 privacy alignment.

* Tip 2: Defend the Limitations
  If the panel says, "Why doesn't the system work offline?" or "Why doesn't it have live 24/7 video chat counseling?"
  Be ready to respond:
  "That is a recognized limitation of our study. To avoid scope creep and keep the project financially sustainable 
  for the school, we restricted the system to text-based submissions and offline-friendly SMS/Email alerting, 
  which fits within the school's existing infrastructure. Expanding to real-time chat or offline syncing is 
  recommended for future research."

* Tip 3: Own the Statistics
  Be prepared to explain the confusion matrix. If the panel asks, "What is more dangerous: a False Positive 
  or a False Negative?"
  Answer:
  "In a crisis intervention system, a False Negative (missing a student in genuine critical distress) is far 
  more dangerous than a False Positive (over-flagging a student). Therefore, we intentionally tuned our 
  classification threshold to be highly sensitive. We implemented the Local Heuristic Fallback in PHP to guarantee 
  we catch explicit critical indicators, and we rely on the guidance counselor's manual review to filter out 
  false alarms."

* Tip 4: Keep Answers Technical and Academic
  Avoid phrases like "We think it works" or "We just copied the code." Always refer back to your literature 
  and methodologies:
  - "Based on the findings of He et al. (2021)..."
  - "In compliance with the Data Privacy Act of 2012..."
  - "Consistent with the Agile SDLC framework we adopted..."
