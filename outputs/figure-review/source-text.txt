NATIONAL TEACHERS COLLEGE School of Arts, Sciences and Technology Bachelor of Science in Information Technology CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION 1.1 Project Context Today’s students grow up in the age where their surroundings are determined primarily by technological connectivity. With the introduction of technology into life, people have gained the chance to get information promptly. The fact that every student and teacher live in the world of screens makes the communication style between them change drastically. Nevertheless, this transition to digital communication has also caused various issues, like cyberbullying, harassment, and even concealed emotional problems. Digital reporting tools can help schools organize concerns disclosed by students and identify submissions that require attention. StudentShield processes vents and bullying reports submitted through its own forms; it does not monitor private messages, social-media accounts, or all student communications. To support Saint Vincent Blessed School of Manila (SVBSM), the developers created StudentShield as a reporting and risk-screening platform. It combines a counselor dashboard with automated SMTP email and SMS notification functions. These functions support staff review; their existence does not establish that bullying or psychological harm has been reduced. 1.2 Purpose and Description The primary goal of StudentShield is to support SVBSM in identifying and responding to emotional distress and reported bullying. Natural language processing screens student-submitted vents, while counselors investigate bullying reports and decide on intervention. In case there is no central monitoring system present at an educational facility, several issues arise when it comes to tracking student behavior and preventing it before it turns out to be very dangerous. That is why the key elements underlying the development of StudentShield are as follows: Proactive Risk Detection: Use NLP and local language rules to screen student-submitted vents for signs requiring counselor attention. Priority Action: Classify vent submissions as Low, Medium, High, or Critical to help counselors prioritize review. Bullying reports follow a separate staff-verification workflow. 1

Project Description StudentShield combines a PHP CodeIgniter 4 web application with a Python FastAPI machine learning service. Students submit vents and incident reports through the web interface, and authorized staff review records through a unified dashboard. The analysis assigns vents to four risk categories: Low, Medium, High, and Critical. High vents trigger counselor notification attempts; Critical vents also trigger parent notification attempts unless the student selects the parent-hiding safety option. Notification delivery depends on the configured services and connectivity. Bullying reports notify counselors at intake and require staff verification before incident alerts are sent to parents. 1.3 Objectives of the Study The primary and specific goals outlined below serve to direct the design, construction, and assessment of StudentShield tailored for Junior High School learners at Saint Vincent Blessed School of Manila (SVBSM). 1.3.1 General Objective To design, develop, and evaluate StudentShield, a web-based emotional-distress screening and bullying-reporting system for Junior High School students at SVBSM, to assist guidance counselors in reviewing student concerns, monitoring risk classifications, and coordinating appropriate follow-up. . 1.3.2 Specific Objectives To create a Safe Space web platform where SVBSM Junior High School students can submit personal vents and confidential bullying reports, with optional photos or videos as incident evidence. Submissions remain linked to authenticated accounts and are not anonymous to authorized staff. To implement risk classification using a fine-tuned mDeBERTa model, a GoEmotions classifier, and multilingual rules that assign Low, Medium, High, or Critical screening categories. 2

BERT-Based Ensemble Learning for Multi-Aspect Hate Speech Detection The proposed combination by Mazari et al. (2023) of the pre-trained BERT with Bidirectional LSTM and Bidirectional GRU neural networks for multiclass detection resulted in an ROC-AUC performance of 98.63%. Instead of classifying the text content based on a general binary label like "toxic/not toxic," their model classified them into different categories such as "threat," "insult," and "identity hate." These researchers relate this technique to the present research in that they both need to categorize the vents of the students not based on only two classes but multiple risk factors such as threats and emotional expression. Following their work, the proponents got the idea of separating the vent into its threat and emotional dimensions, giving counselors a clear view of the risks involved.
