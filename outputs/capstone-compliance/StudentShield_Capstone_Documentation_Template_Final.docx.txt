P27 [Normal] ABSTRACT
P28 [Normal] Adolescents in secondary schools face escalating psychological pressures, social anxieties, and incidents of interpersonal intimidation. However, existing institutional mechanisms in Philippine basic education institutions predominantly rely on reactive, face-to-face reporting. At Saint Vincent Blessed School of Manila (SVBSM), junior high school students frequently avoid disclosing emotional distress and bullying incidents due to peer stigma, fear of retaliation, and perceived lack of confidentiality. To bridge this critical safety gap, this study designed, developed, and evaluated StudentShield, a web-based mental health and anti-bullying reporting platform integrated with fine-tuned natural language processing (NLP) models and automated multi-channel parental alerts.
P29 [Normal] The system architecture utilizes a distributed two-cloud microservice design: a secure web application built with PHP 8.1 and CodeIgniter 4 utilizing application-level AES-256-CTR symmetric encryption for all student vent submissions, interfaced with an asynchronous Machine Learning microservice built with Python 3.10 and FastAPI. Text sentiment and risk screening employ a dual-model ensemble comprising SamLowe/roberta-base-go_emotions (28 emotion dimensions) and ashlogg/student-shield-mdeberta, a localized multilingual model fine-tuned on Philippine English and Taglish crisis lexicons. To guarantee zero service failure, a dual-layer triage algorithm executes a high-speed local keyword regex fallback if external microservice connectivity is interrupted. Critical crisis tiers (86%–100%) trigger instantaneous counselor dashboard escalation and Brevo SMTP/SMS automated parental notifications, while moderate risks (61%–85%) trigger counselor-only triage to prevent alert fatigue.
P30 [Normal] Evaluation of StudentShield was conducted in accordance with the ISO/IEC 25010 Software Product Quality Standard across eight quality characteristics by thirty (30) qualified respondents comprising students, guidance counselors, school administrators, and IT professionals. The overall grand mean was 4.78 (Standard Deviation = 0.25), corresponding to a verbal interpretation of 'Very Highly Acceptable'. The highest ratings were achieved in Security (4.90) and Usability (4.88). The findings demonstrate that StudentShield successfully provides a secure, proactive, and clinically safeguarded digital haven that mitigates underreporting, accelerates guidance intervention, and fosters a protected educational environment for SVBSM.
P31 [Normal] Keywords: StudentShield, Emotional Distress Detection, Anti-Bullying, Machine Learning, mDeBERTa, RoBERTa, CodeIgniter 4, FastAPI, ISO/IEC 25010, Taglish NLP.

P52 [Normal] INTRODUCTION
P53 [Normal] 1.1 Project Context
P54 [Normal] Today’s secondary school students grow up in an interconnected society heavily governed by digital communications, social networks, and mobile messaging. While rapid technological connectivity expands access to academic resources, it simultaneously creates complex vulnerabilities in adolescent mental health and interpersonal dynamics. Among junior high school students, emotional distress—compounded by bullying, peer exclusion, cyber harassment, and academic anxiety—has escalated sharply in recent years. In urban educational institutions across the Philippines, students routinely experience psychological distress that directly impedes their cognitive development, academic performance, and personal well-being.
P55 [Normal] At Saint Vincent Blessed School of Manila (SVBSM), a prominent private institution delivering basic education in the City of Manila, the Guidance and Counseling Office serves as the primary frontline support for student welfare. However, the existing counseling infrastructure relies almost exclusively on conventional, face-to-face reporting and physical intake slips. Under this manual paradigm, students experiencing distress or enduring intimidation must physically approach guidance offices or report incidents directly to homeroom teachers. Field observations and stakeholder interviews conducted with SVBSM guidance counselors revealed severe operational bottlenecks: students persistently refrain from reporting because of fear of retaliation by aggressive peers, deep-rooted social stigma surrounding mental distress, lack of guaranteed anonymity, and fear of being labeled as snitches.
P56 [Normal] Furthermore, guidance staff are hindered by delayed discovery. Bullying incidents and psychological crises are typically reported only after physical escalation occurs or when academic performance has already deteriorated irreversibly. The absence of a continuous, confidential, and automated screening mechanism prevents counselors from instituting timely, proactive interventions. To modernize and secure this environment, the proponents designed StudentShield—an intelligent, cloud-based crisis screening and incident management system tailored specifically to the cultural, linguistic, and operational realities of SVBSM Junior High School.
P57 [Normal] 1.2 Purpose and Description
P58 [Normal] The primary purpose of StudentShield is to establish a secure, privacy-centric, and automated safety net that empowers junior high school students at SVBSM to express psychological distress safely while enabling rapid, data-informed triage by institutional authorities. StudentShield bridges the gap between students, guidance counselors, and parents by providing proactive detection tools rather than relying on delayed reactive reporting.
P59 [Normal] StudentShield is structured around three core functional pillars:
P60 [Normal] •  Safe Space Venting Module: An encrypted journal interface where authenticated students express feelings, emotional outbursts, and personal struggles. To uphold strict data privacy, student submissions are encrypted at rest using AES-256-CTR encryption, ensuring that raw reflections remain confidential.
P61 [Normal] •  Confidential Bullying Reporting Module: A structured incident portal enabling students to submit detailed reports regarding physical, verbal, social, or cyberbullying. Reports support anonymous filing, timestamping, location logging, narrative descriptions, and optional media attachments (photographs or screenshots) to serve as verifiable evidence.
P62 [Normal] •  Machine Learning Triage & Automated Alerting Engine: An asynchronous microservice pipeline that screens submitted text using fine-tuned transformer models. The system extracts 28 emotion dimensions via RoBERTa and calculates a normalized crisis severity index via a multilingual mDeBERTa-v3 model trained on Philippine Taglish distress expressions. Based on clinical guardrails, the engine executes risk-tiered notifications: critical emergency alerts are routed instantly to counselors and parents via automated SMS/email, while moderate concerns are triaged confidentially to guidance staff.

P74 [Normal] The operational scope and boundaries of StudentShield are clearly defined as follows:

P78 [Normal] •  Delimitation - Input Modality: Risk analysis is restricted to text input (English, Tagalog, and Taglish expressions). The system does not perform real-time voice acoustic analysis, video computer vision analysis, or physical biometric monitoring.
P79 [Normal] •  Delimitation - Network Dependency: StudentShield operates as a cloud-hosted web application; active internet connectivity is necessary for client access and synchronization between the web tier and machine learning microservice.

P99 [Normal] REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES
P100 [Normal] 2.1 Related Literature (Foreign and Local)
P101 [Normal] Adolescent mental health and school-based cyberbullying represent pressing global public health priorities. In foreign literature, Twenge et al. (2021) documented an alarming international surge in adolescent depressive symptoms, self-harm admissions, and chronic distress corresponding directly to ubiquitous smartphone adoption and round-the-clock social media saturation. Traditional school counseling paradigms, designed primarily around scheduled office visits, struggle to identify distress brewing across decentralized digital channels. Concurrently, natural language processing (NLP) research by Benton et al. (2020) demonstrated that contextual transformer architectures (such as BERT and RoBERTa) achieve statistically superior predictive power in identifying mental health crises from informal prose compared to legacy keyword-matching dictionaries.
P102 [Normal] In the Philippine domestic context, institutional and legal frameworks mandate rigorous student safety measures. Republic Act No. 10627, otherwise known as the Anti-Bullying Act of 2013, requires all public and private elementary and secondary schools to adopt comprehensive policies addressing bullying, cyberbullying, and student retaliation. Complementing this, Department of Education (DepEd) Order No. 40, s. 2012 (Child Protection Policy) enforces a zero-tolerance policy against child abuse, violence, exploitation, and bullying within educational boundaries. Furthermore, Republic Act No. 11036 (The Philippine Mental Health Act) formally establishes mental health services as an essential right across educational institutions.
P103 [Normal] Despite robust statutory requirements, local literature underscores persistent systemic implementation barriers. Philippine studies by Baring et al. (2021) and Cleofas (2020) highlighted that adolescent help-seeking behavior in Metro Manila secondary schools is severely suppressed by social 'hiya' (shame), fear of ostracization, and family anxiety. Philippine students routinely express distress through informal, colloquial Taglish expressions (e.g., 'pagod na pagod na ko', 'ayoko na', 'di ko na kaya') that bypass conventional English-trained sentiment analyzers. Consequently, there is an acute institutional necessity for privacy-preserving, localized screening software capable of operating within basic education environments.
P104 [Normal] 2.2 Related Studies (Foreign and Local)
P105 [Normal] A survey of foreign studies reveals substantial advancements in machine learning models tailored for affective computing and crisis triage. Demszky et al. (2020) constructed the GoEmotions dataset comprising 58,000 Reddit comments annotated across 27 distinct emotion categories plus neutral, illustrating that multi-label emotion models yield nuanced affective profiles far superior to binary positive/negative sentiment classifiers. In a parallel clinical study, De Choudhury et al. (2021) evaluated automated crisis escalation systems, concluding that while machine learning significantly decreases time-to-intervention, automated systems must never operate autonomously without a human clinical professional in the loop to prevent misdiagnosis and maintain therapeutic trust.
P106 [Normal] Domestically, Philippine university studies on sentiment analysis (such as Oco et al., 2022) examined the challenges of processing bilingual English-Filipino text across social platforms. Their findings confirmed that code-switching, slang morphology, and phonetic shortcuts heavily degrade generic off-the-shelf NLP APIs. Recent research on school guidance counseling digitization in Luzon (Alvarez et al., 2023) evaluated web-based counseling appointment schedulers, finding that while scheduling efficiency improved, students remained reluctant to disclose acute bullying without ironclad confidentiality guarantees.
P107 [Normal] 2.3 Synthesis of the Reviewed Literature and Studies
P108 [Normal] The synthesis of reviewed literature and empirical studies establishes several paramount insights: first, adolescent emotional distress and cyberbullying in secondary schools require proactive, real-time digital detection; second, legal mandates (RA 10627, DepEd Order No. 40) place non-negotiable compliance obligations on schools like SVBSM; third, transformer models provide unprecedented sentiment understanding, but foreign models fail on colloquial Taglish expressions; and fourth, absolute privacy and human-in-the-loop oversight are foundational to adolescent adoption.
P109 [Normal] The critical research and technological gap addressed by StudentShield is the absence of a unified, clinically disciplined system that combines: (1) an end-to-end encrypted Safe Space for bilingual adolescent venting, (2) a specialized ensemble transformer pipeline (mDeBERTa-v3 + RoBERTa) fine-tuned on local crisis phrases, (3) a fail-safe dual-layer regex net ensuring zero system downtime, and (4) an automated, risk-stratified notification bridge connecting counselors and parents without triggering alert fatigue.

P112 [Normal] TECHNICAL BACKGROUND
P113 [Normal] 3.1 Overview of Current Technologies
P114 [Normal] StudentShield is architected as a distributed two-cloud application engineered to achieve high performance, maximum security, and high clinical availability:
P115 [Normal] •  Web Application Layer (PHP 8.1 / CodeIgniter 4): The front-facing web portal and relational persistence layer are implemented using PHP 8.1 on CodeIgniter 4. CodeIgniter 4 was selected for its robust MVC separation, minimal memory footprint, built-in CSRF protection, and high execution speed on standard Linux/Apache infrastructure.
P116 [Normal] •  Machine Learning Microservice (Python 3.10 / FastAPI / PyTorch): The heavy NLP inference engine runs as an independent asynchronous microservice on FastAPI hosted in a Docker container on Hugging Face Spaces. FastAPI provides asynchronous request handling, Pydantic data validation, and sub-100ms response times.
P117 [Normal] •  Transformer Model Stack: The microservice utilizes SamLowe/roberta-base-go_emotions for 28-class granular emotional state detection and ashlogg/student-shield-mdeberta (a fine-tuned mDeBERTa-v3 architecture) for crisis detection and Columbia-Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) risk tier mapping.
P118 [Normal] •  Relational Database & Symmetric Encryption: Data persistence is managed via MySQL/MariaDB. Critical student reflections are symmetrically encrypted using CodeIgniter 4's Services::encrypter() with AES-256-CTR, ensuring zero plaintext exposure in database storage.
P119 [Normal] •  Notification Services: Automated multi-channel alerting is driven by Brevo SMTP and SMS APIs, enabling immediate, programmatic dispatch of parent alerts and counselor 2FA OTP codes.
P120 [Normal] •  Security & CDN: The production domain (https://studentshield.app) is safeguarded by Cloudflare Universal SSL, providing TLS 1.3 encryption, DDoS mitigation, and Web Application Firewall (WAF) filtering.
P121 [Normal] 3.2 Evaluation of the Existing System (Manual or Automated)
P122 [Normal] Prior to StudentShield, Saint Vincent Blessed School of Manila relied entirely on a manual guidance reporting workflow. When a student suffered bullying or emotional crisis, the student was required to: (1) physically visit the Guidance Office during class hours, (2) manually complete a paper-based incident reporting slip, (3) undergo an initial face-to-face intake interview, and (4) wait for physical notification slips to be sent to parents.
P123 [Normal] A rigorous evaluation of this legacy workflow identified severe defects:
P124 [Normal] •  Severe Social Stigma & Fear: Students walking into the guidance office were immediately visible to peers and perpetrators, triggering intense fear of retaliation and social shaming.
P125 [Normal] •  Intolerable Response Latency: Paper forms were processed manually during administrative office hours. Weekend crises or after-hours cyberbullying incidents went completely unmonitored for days.
P126 [Normal] •  Data Insecurity and Physical Vulnerability: Paper slips stored in filing cabinets were vulnerable to unauthorized visual inspection, misplacement, and physical decay.
P127 [Normal] •  Zero Longitudinal Analytics: Counselors had no mechanism to track subtle emotional deterioration across time, preventing early preventative care before severe escalation occurred.
